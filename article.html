The 10-Minute Matcha Ritual

Updated: Oct 24 · 4 min read

You do not need a free morning. You need ten minutes, a bowl, and a whisk.

🍵

Why a ritual beats a routine

A routine is a to-do list. A ritual is something you do slowly, on purpose, and notice while you do it. Making matcha is perfect for this because it cannot be rushed. The water has to cool a little, the powder has to be sifted, and the whisking takes real attention.

What you need

  • 1 tsp good matcha powder
  • 70 ml hot water, around 80°C (not boiling)
  • A small bowl and a bamboo whisk, or a fork if you are starting out

The ten minutes

Sift the matcha into the bowl so there are no clumps. Pour in the water and whisk in a quick "W" motion until a fine foam covers the top. Then sit down. Drink it slowly, with no phone in your other hand.

The tea is only half of it. The pause is the other half.
🌿

Make it stick

Pick one fixed spot, like the window seat or the balcony step, and always drink it there. Your brain will start linking that place with calm, and after a week or two the ritual begins to feel automatic.

A Reading Nook Anywhere

Updated: Oct 20 · 3 min read

A nook is less about space and more about a signal that says: this is where I slow down.

📚

Start with the corner, not the furniture

Look for a spot that already feels a little tucked away: beside a bookshelf, under a window, or next to the bed. You are borrowing a corner, not building a room.

The three-item kit

  • Something soft to sit on, such as a floor cushion or an old armchair
  • Warm light from a small lamp, not the main ceiling light
  • A blanket you only use here
Keep one book on the cushion at all times, so starting takes zero effort.
🛋️

Protect it

The one rule: no laptop and no scrolling in the nook. Once the space is only for reading, sitting down starts to feel like exhaling.
